The blue economy seeks to reconcile sea-related economic activities with environmental conservation and the well-being of communities.

The economic value produced by the oceans depends on maintaining **healthy ecosystems**.

The degradation of mangroves, reefs, estuaries and fish stocks undermines the very economic base of maritime activities.

Economic growth and conservation should not be treated as separate goals. **Sustainability is a condition** for the continued generation of income.

## From concept to policy

- The term gained traction after the Rio+20 Conference (2012) and Sustainable Development Goal 14 (Life Below Water), which sets targets for fisheries, pollution, acidification and protected areas.
- Mangroves and estuaries work as nurseries for commercial species: protecting them is an investment in the future productivity of fisheries — not merely an environmental cost.
- Marine protected areas, closed seasons and marine spatial planning are instruments that only work with data, enforcement and the participation of those who live from the sea; one of the projects linked to this series measures precisely the effects of such measures on the Brazilian coast.
