Maritime economics and fisheries economics study activities that use the sea and its resources as a productive, logistical and social base.

The subject covers shipping, ports, fishing, processing, infrastructure, energy, trade, science, environmental monitoring and traditional communities.

The series sets out to show that sea-related economic development depends on integrating **productive efficiency**, **social inclusion**, **scientific knowledge** and **sustainability**.

## The route of the series

1. Maritime economics goes well beyond ships.
2. Shipping routes shape global costs.
3. Ports and territorial development.
4. The blue economy: growing without degrading.
5. Small-scale and industrial fishing.
6. Fisheries as a common-pool resource.
7. Fisheries bioeconomics.
8. From sea to market: the seafood value chain.
9. Data for sustainable fisheries.

## Further reading

- OECD (2016). *The Ocean Economy in 2030*. Paris: OECD Publishing. [doi:10.1787/9789264251724-en](https://doi.org/10.1787/9789264251724-en) — Estimates of the economic weight of the oceans and scenarios for the sector.
- FAO (2024). *The State of World Fisheries and Aquaculture 2024 — Blue Transformation in Action*. Rome: FAO. [doi:10.4060/cd0683en](https://doi.org/10.4060/cd0683en) — The biennial reference report on world fisheries and aquaculture.
- UNCTAD (2024). *Review of Maritime Transport 2024*. Geneva: United Nations. [Report](https://unctad.org/publication/review-maritime-transport-2024) — The annual overview of shipping and ports.
- Stopford, M. (2009). *Maritime Economics*. 3rd ed. London: Routledge. — The classic maritime economics textbook.
- Anderson, L. G.; Seijo, J. C. (2010). *Bioeconomics of Fisheries Management*. Ames: Wiley-Blackwell. — An introduction to fisheries economics through bioeconomic models.
