The series presents the economic foundations needed to understand the digital economy: information goods, network effects, multi-sided platforms, the new monetisation models and the competition and regulatory challenges they raise.

The content was developed to bring economic concepts closer to the daily reality of professionals and students in information systems, law, public administration and related fields — and it grows out of the Digital Economy and Digital Law courses I teach in the Information Systems programme at UFAL.

The central premise is that decisions about software, data, system architecture and platform design are not merely technical choices. They also shape business models, incentives, competition, users' rights and economic outcomes.

## How to measure it: models and methods

The concepts in the series have empirical counterparts. These two panels, first published on LinkedIn, summarise the families of models I use in research and in the classroom to turn the ideas into measurements.

![Panel "Econometric models: network economics": graph theory and social network analysis (SNA), which maps relationships and measures centrality and influence; network externality models (Metcalfe's law), which quantify the additional value of each new user; and multi-sided platform (matchmaking) models, which study pricing, cross-subsidies and value orchestration in two-sided markets.](./modelos-economia-de-redes.webp)

![Panel "Models in the digital economy": dynamic pricing (real-time price adjustment driven by supply, demand and algorithms); demand forecasting with time series (ARIMA, LSTM) for resource and infrastructure management; and data valuation and impact assessment with econometric analysis (difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity and data valuation) to measure efficiency and technology adoption.](./modelos-economia-digital.webp)

- **Graph theory and social network analysis (SNA)** — maps relationships and measures centrality and influence; essential to understand the structure and flow of information.
- **Network externalities (Metcalfe's law)** — quantifies the additional value generated by each new user and analyses network growth.
- **Multi-sided platforms (matchmaking)** — studies two-sided markets, focusing on pricing, cross-subsidies and value orchestration.
- **Dynamic pricing** — real-time price adjustment based on supply, demand and algorithms, with revenue optimisation.
- **Demand forecasting** — time series (ARIMA, LSTM) applied to resource and infrastructure management, such as cloud traffic.
- **Data valuation and impact evaluation** — difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity and data valuation to measure efficiency and technology adoption.
