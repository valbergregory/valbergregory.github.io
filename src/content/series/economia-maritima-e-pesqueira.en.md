The sea is a piece of economic infrastructure that connects production, trade, food, energy, logistics, communities and territorial development. Maritime economics examines the activities associated with the oceans, transport, ports, logistics chains, natural resources and coastal territories. Fisheries economics concentrates on the use of fishery resources, the behaviour of agents, the costs of the activity, the formation of income, the conservation of stocks and the instruments of management and regulation.

My interest in these subjects took shape during the first semester of 2026, when I had the opportunity to teach Fisheries Economics to a class in the Fisheries Engineering programme at the Federal University of Alagoas. Despite health setbacks during the semester, we completed the planned work satisfactorily. Living alongside the students and getting closer to researchers and to the concrete problems of fishing broadened my learning and awakened a lasting identification with the field.

From that experience I gradually began to develop research, teaching materials, scientific articles and applied-project proposals related to maritime and fisheries economics. The publications are in dialogue with ongoing studies on marine heatwaves and fishing effort, the effects of closed seasons and marine protected areas, and the performance of Brazilian ports. As they are completed, course materials and applied products aimed at the fishing communities of Alagoas and the lower São Francisco will also be added to the site.

Thus, the series starts from the conviction that sea-related development depends on the articulation between scientific knowledge, economic efficiency, environmental protection and community participation. Studying these relationships is indispensable for turning maritime and fisheries potential into lasting income, food security and territorial development.
