## Where and what I teach

I am a professor at the Federal University of Alagoas, Penedo Educational Unit, in the Information Systems programme, where I also sit on the programme board and the curriculum core group (NDE). My courses lie where management, economics and law meet technology: Introduction to Economics, Accounting Information for Management, Introduction to Management, Law Applied to Computing, Small Business and Startup Management, English for Specific Purposes and Team Management and Leadership. I also teach in other programmes of the unit — Quality Management in Tourism, in the Tourism programme, and Fisheries Economics, in Fisheries Engineering — and, between 2019 and 2021, I was a substitute lecturer in the Tourism programme, teaching mathematics, economics and financial and accounting management applied to the sector.

## The classroom as the source of questions

Contact with students is what turns concepts into concrete questions. Explaining elasticity to Tourism students leads, almost inevitably, to the question of what makes a foreign tourist choose Brazil; discussing public goods with future fisheries engineers leads to seasonal closures and marine protected areas; teaching data protection to Information Systems students leads to the governance of the AI systems that courts are adopting.

Some of these questions became permanent research fronts:

- **Fisheries Economics** — teaching the subject to Fisheries Engineering students in 2026 opened a work programme on marine heatwaves, fishing effort, closures and protected areas, using vessel-tracking data and a geospatial data cube.
- **Tourism Economics** — work with the Tourism programme strengthened my interest in international demand, air connectivity and the territorial development of Penedo and the Lower São Francisco.
- **Jurimetrics, AI and digital government** — teaching subjects that connect Law, Economics and technology drives the projects on court decisions as data, AI adoption in the judiciary and the digitalisation of public processes.

## The way back

Research and outreach return to the classroom as real data, case studies and tools. The datasets built for the papers — milk prices, port calls, fishing effort, court decisions — become exercises and course projects; the R and Python pipelines become material for outreach courses on data analysis; and the dashboards planned as research outputs are designed so that students take part in building and maintaining them.

I supervise final-year projects on topics such as business intelligence, IT governance, information systems for the public sector and the monitoring of photovoltaic systems, and I serve on examination boards in the Information Systems and Economics programmes.
